I came to know about the existence and influence of Contrarian ideas in life and business while reading Peter Thiel's
[Zero to One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_to_One). After that, I started looking for Ideas or Paradoxes we use in everyday life that run against our intuition. The following are some of the Paradoxes assimilated in my collection. I am not the original author of this work. This is an attempt to collect some of the Contrarian Ideas encountered in one place.

The nature of these ideas instills a thought that sometimes you have to go "South" to go "North". A related mental model is the [Law of Diminishing Returns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diminishing_returns), where the yield starts going down even as more effort is applied. Looking for opportunities to question things in life and apply them helped with better results avoiding a lot of embarrassment and anxiety.

Use them more like the [rules of thumb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_thumb) and not like the empirical laws of Physics. They are not always black and white. There are no proofs either. It's more of an art than science. But being aware of them helps avoid regrettable outcomes.


<a id="org8459c36"></a>

## Backward Law

**Stop looking in order to find what you are looking for**. Trying harder does not help but hinder progress. When solving complex problems, giving a break or taking a walk helps more than continuing to solve the problem. Too much focus on getting the next Promotion, clearing the interviews and achieving single-pointed focus during meditation sessions makes each of them less likely to happen. At times, the more focused you are on the destination the more you move away from it.


<a id="org7ec4010"></a>

## Speed Paradox

There are times when you have to **slow down to speed up**. Taking time to structure thoughts in pencil / paper rather than jumping into writing code helps to build the system quicker.


<a id="orge564895"></a>

## Effort Paradox

**More effort to make things appear effortless**. It took 17 years 114 days of hard work for Lionel Messi to become an overnight wonder. We only see the tip of the iceberg. There's a large "effort" surface hidden beneath in appearing effortless.


<a id="orgb3f30af"></a>

## Opportunity Paradox

**Take on less, accomplish more**. This is a direct example of the Law of diminishing returns. We assume most things in life to follow a [Uniform Distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_uniform_distribution), but alas they tend to follow [Pareto Principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_principle) (or the 80 / 20 rule). Not all tasks are created equal. Like stocks, identify the high-value tasks and invest your efforts in them to get better returns.


<a id="org0f4e212"></a>

## Productivity Paradox

**Work longer, accomplish less**. Most of the management principles we use were created during the Industrial Revolution
powering the Production Lines / Factories. The more time a worker spends in the assembly line, the more they produce. In the software world or solving complex math problems this does not directly apply. In these disciplines, **quality trumps quantity**. Most of the time a few quality timeboxes win over spending uninterrupted long hours in front of the computer.


<a id="org3a69cc5"></a>

## Growth / Investment Paradox

**Growth happens gradually and then suddenly**. Growth in life and business takes longer than you think even if you know the Growth Paradox. But when the breakthrough happens it happens much faster than we can imagine. The classic example is the modern day AI systems. They went through a lot of winters before achieving the tremendous results in business, coding and in everyday life. There's a lot of time spent in the incubation period before the inflection point is reached. After that, it accelerates in an exponential trajectory.


<a id="orgf43dbbc"></a>

## Desire Paradox

This one is my personal favorite. **Desire leads to more achievement but also more suffering**. Uncontrolled desire
distracts from **the beauty of having enough**. Pursue growth but never let the chase for more blind you to the value of
what you already have. Know when to stop.


<a id="orgea90d39"></a>

## Fear Paradox

**The things we fear the most are often the ones we want the most in life**. I personally feared criticism and never
published my web logs. The result, having a pile of notes / logs that I have been sitting on without publishing. Unless I overcome this fear, I would never be able to get the "constructive" feedback and form community with the like-minded people.


<a id="orgbf9d9ef"></a>

## Death Paradox

**Know your death in order to truly live**. Understanding that life in this world is ephemeral helps you make better choices.


<a id="org4f257a2"></a>

## Conclusion

Though some of the paradoxes intersect more, I classified them under different sections for **better understanding and absorption**. Being aware of them, internalizing and then leveraging them will really help towards making better decisions in software and life in general.
